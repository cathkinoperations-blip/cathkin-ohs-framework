Document Ref: OHS-MOD-001 Regulatory Standard: OHS Act (Act 85 of 1993) §16.1 & §16.2 Workplace Scope: Small Workplace (<20 Employees)

1. Executive Summary & Legal Framework

Under Section 16(1) of the Occupational Health and Safety Act (Act 85 of 1993), ultimate legal liability for health, safety, and workplace environmental compliance rests with the Chief Executive Officer or Board Chairman of Cathkin Estates HOA.

To ensure effective operational management across estate maintenance, contractor activities, and common property, Section 16(2) provides the statutory mechanism to delegate specific health and safety duties to competent personnel (such as the Estate Manager or designated Operations Lead).

LEGAL GOVERNANCE & STATUTORY HIERARCHY MODEL
Cathkin Estates HOA maintains a clear vertical statutory chain of command running directly from Homeowners through the Board (§16.1), branching out to oversight committees (Finance, HSSE, and Architectural), and flowing down to the Estate Manager (§16.2) and Developers & Contractors (§37.2).

5. OHS Governance & Committee Framework

Strategic oversight is maintained across the specialized governance pillars (HSSE, Finance, and Architectural Review), while the primary vertical chain of command runs from Homeowners through the Board (§16.1) down to the Estate Manager (§16.2) and Contractors (§37.2).

SECTION 2.1: EXPLANATORY NOTES FOR THE GOVERNANCE ORGANIGRAMME

1. Purpose of the Framework

This organigramme defines the operational hierarchy, reporting lines, and statutory divisions of power within the Cathkin Estates Homeowners Association. It moves away from centralized individual oversight to establish a modern governance framework based on the segregation of duties, collective board liability, and proactive risk management.

2. Structural Breakdown & Flow of Authority

Tier 1: The Supreme Authority – Members (Homeowners / AGM)
Status: Ultimate Accountable Body.
Role: Sitting at the apex of the structure, the Members hold ultimate decision-making power. Through the Annual General Meeting (AGM), they elect the Board of Directors, approve the global annual budget, and amend the founding Memorandum of Incorporation (MOI) or Constitution.

Tier 2: The Executive Authority – The Board of Directors
Status: Collective Fiduciary Body.
Role: The Board acts as a collective unit. No single individual, including the Chairman, holds unilateral executive authority. All strategic decisions, major expenditures, and rule adoptions require a formal board vote and resolution.
Statutory Alignment: Holds ultimate, non-delegable corporate liability under Section 76 of the Companies Act and criminal health and safety liability under Section 16(1) of the Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) Act.

Tier 3: The Regulatory Filter – Horizontal Committees & Portfolios
Before any strategic instruction reaches the operational team, it must pass through one of three horizontal regulatory gates. This ensures technical vetting, fiscal discipline, and strict legislative compliance:

  • A. Finance Committee (Formal Sub-Committee):
    Nature: A formally constituted advisory body composed of directors and volunteer financial experts from the community.
    Function: Responsible for budgetary allocations, long-term capital reserve calculations, financial audits, and fiscal policy. It implements a strict Delegation of Authority (DoA) matrix, restricting spending power within predefined budgetary limits.
  • B. HSSE / Operations Portfolio (Direct Board Assignment):
    Nature: An internal board portfolio managed directly by a designated director rather than an independent administrative committee.
    Function: Retains hands-on board oversight regarding workplace safety, estate environmental preservation, and liability minimization. It ensures that infrastructure risks are prioritized within the estate budget.
  • C. Architectural Review Committee (Formal Sub-Committee):
    Nature: A formal committee enforcing aesthetic and technical structural conformity across the estate.
    Function: Vets building plans, construction variations, and structural alterations against estate guidelines, ensuring compliance with the National Building Regulations (Act 103 of 1977) before municipal submission.

Tier 4: The Operational Execution – Estate Manager
Status: Executive Operations.
Role: Sits beneath the regulatory gate to run day-to-day estate management, infrastructure up-keep, and rule enforcement. The Estate Manager acts on the collective policies of the Board, not on individual requests from single directors.
Statutory Alignment: Receives formal, written delegation from the Board under Section 16(2) of the OHS Act to act as the on-site competent safety head.

Tier 5: The Ground Level – Developers & Contractors (Builders of Houses)
Status: Third-Party Service Providers[cite: 3].
Role: The boots on the ground executing structural development, home construction, security services, landscaping, and maintenance.
Statutory Alignment: Explicitly treated as external third-party entities strictly insulated from the estate’s internal liability pool[cite: 3]. This insulation is enforced through mandatory Section 37(2) written health and safety agreements and verified COIDA (Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act) compliance files managed directly by the Estate Manager[cite: 3].
Framework Amendments: To eliminate liability leakage, developer and builder operations require strict pre-commencement approval gates (site-specific HIRA and Section 37(2) compliance), active periodic independent safety and environmental audits during construction, and explicit linkage to refundable building deposits and infrastructure damage bonds for common property protection.
